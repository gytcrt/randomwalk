{{< newtabref href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dream_of_the_Red_Chamber" title="Honglou Meng (Dream of the Red Chamber)" >}} is to Chinese as  War and Peace is to Russians and A Hundred Years of Solitude is to Colombians. Although I doubt Honglou Meng is as widely known by non-Chinese readers as the other two canonical works of literature. It is even true that most educated Chinese readers in the 21st century have not read the original book. Written in the 18th century during the Qing Dynasty, the influence of Honglou Meng has been largely spread by educated elites, theater, and movie and TV adaptations. Nevertheless, almost every Chinese speaking person knows Honglou Meng’s protagonists Lin Daiyu and Jia Baoyu as cultural characters. 

Honglou Meng is challenging to read even for a college-educated Chinese reader, since it’s long and written in Chinese that was used 300 years ago. The first 80 chapters alone come to 600,000 Chinese characters, which roughly translates to a half million English words. Much of the dialogue reflects how people spoke in the Qing Dynasty, and classical Chinese verses appear frequently in the book as well. Similar to a modern English speaker reading Shakespeare today, one can generally follow the story but may struggle with comprehending the verses and the allusions.

{{< figurelightbox src="hongloumeng-version.jpg" caption="The Red Inkstone (Zhiyanzhai) commentary edition I'm reading: traditional Chinese, with commentary." align="center" width="100%" >}}

I first started reading Honglou Meng when I was about 12, and it was honestly not fun. I powered through the first 20 chapters then gave up. The language was difficult and I was too young to understand the motifs and dynamics of characters in the book. 

Twenty years later, I eventually finished and fell in love with Honglou Meng—I consider it the greatest novel I’ve ever read and so ahead of its time. However, I still struggle with verses in the book, and so do my other Honglou Meng fan friends.

Therefore, I created two small sites to help readers understand the verses in Honglou Meng and learn traditional Chinese poetry following Lin Daiyu’s guidance in the book. The two sites should work smoothly on both laptop and phone without downloading any app or sign-up.

《红楼梦》之于华人就像《战争与和平》之于俄罗斯人或者《百年孤独》之于哥伦比亚人。遗憾的是相较于这两本经典，中文世界之外的读者对于《红楼梦》知之甚少。我甚至认为读过《红楼梦》原书华人也是极为有限的。写于清代的《红楼梦》在华人世界的影响力是几百年来通过文人贵族、戏剧、电影、电视改编来传播的。但毋庸置疑的是，几乎所有华人都知道林黛玉和贾宝玉的形象和性格。

《红楼梦》对于当代人来说很难读——太长了，且语言和现代汉语有隔阂。我现在所读脂砚斋本前八十回就有洋洋洒洒四大本五十万字，其语言是清代白话夹杂各种诗词文言。普通读者虽能通过清朝人说话方式和诗词文言大致理解剧情，但其细微之处确实不易懂。

{{< figurelightbox src="hongloumeng-version.jpg" caption="我正在读的脂砚斋评本：繁体，附脂批。" align="center" width="100%" >}}

我第一次读《红楼梦》大概十二岁，平心而论，阅读体验不佳。我凭着好胜之心一直读到二十来回才放弃。对于没有学过文言的我来说，理解书中的语言是一难；其二，此时的我也不经世事，理解书中的人情世故又是一难。

时过境迁，直到今年我才爱上和重新读完《红楼梦》。不止如此，它已跃升为我心中最伟大的小说。可我还是无法轻松理解和欣赏其中的诗词和典故。问了一遍我周围其他的《红楼梦》迷们，他们基本也和我有同样的困境。

所以我有空就开发了两个小站，让大家能够看到红楼梦每个章回诗词歌赋的白话和背景解释，且能够像香菱一样跟着林黛玉学诗。无论用手机或电脑，用浏览器打开就可以阅读，不需下载任何软件或注册。
